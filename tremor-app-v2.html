Neuroscience

What causes Parkinson's tremor?

Tremor originates from disrupted signalling between the basal ganglia and thalamus. Loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra creates pathological oscillations at 3–6 Hz — precisely the range TremorCare's bandpass filter targets.

Classification

Types of tremor in PD

  • Resting tremor (3–6 Hz) — hallmark of PD; "pill-rolling" disappears during movement
  • Postural tremor — when holding position against gravity
  • Re-emergent tremor — appears 8–10s after outstretching arms
  • Task-specific tremor — triggered by specific activities like writing
Treatment

Evidence-based treatments

  • Levodopa/carbidopa — first-line; moderate tremor effect
  • Deep Brain Stimulation (DBS) — VIM nucleus; highly effective for refractory tremor
  • Focused Ultrasound (FUS) — non-invasive thalamotomy for unilateral tremor
  • Tai chi, boxing, cycling — evidence for motor and non-motor benefits
Triggers

What worsens tremor severity

  • Stress & anxiety — most consistently reported trigger
  • Fatigue & poor sleep — REM disruption very common in PD
  • Medication off-states — most predictable and treatable cause
  • Caffeine — variable; may worsen in sensitive individuals
Recent research 2022–2024
2024

Wearable IMU tremor monitoring: clinical validation

Multi-centre study validated MPU-6050-based devices vs MDS-UPDRS III. 3–6 Hz RMS correlated strongly (r=0.87) with clinician-rated severity.

J. Parkinson's Disease · DOI: 10.3233/JPD-240018
2023

ML prediction of levodopa "off" states

91% sensitivity predicting off-periods 45 min before onset using RMS amplitude and frequency drift from wearable sensors.

npj Digital Medicine · DOI: 10.1038/s41746-023-00832-1
2023

HIIT slows motor progression in early PD

128 participants: HIIT significantly slowed MDS-UPDRS III progression vs. moderate exercise at 6 months.

NEJM Evidence · DOI: 10.1056/EVIDoa2300063
2022

FUS thalamotomy vs. DBS: comparative review

Meta-analysis (n=1,842): both achieved >70% tremor reduction. FUS: non-invasive. DBS: bilateral, adjustable, reversible.

The Lancet Neurology · DOI: 10.1016/S1474-4422(22)00162-6
Warning signs

When to contact your neurologist

Seek prompt attention if you notice: significant increase in tremor amplitude over 2–4 weeks; tremor spreading to new areas; sudden worsening; falls; or new cognitive symptoms.

Note: TremorCare is a monitoring aid, not a diagnostic device. Always consult your neurologist for medical decisions.
